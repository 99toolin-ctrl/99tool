What is the Infinite Reaction Tester?

The Infinite Reaction Tester is a millisecond-precision visual reflex analyzer designed to measure your response time to sudden color shifts. Reaction time represents the time duration between the presentation of a sensory stimulus and the execution of a corresponding motor response. Visual processing speed is critical for fast-paced coordination in sports, esports, and driving safety.

This utility uses high-precision timers (`performance.now()`) to track visual latency without background lag. By recording response values instantly in your local browser sandbox, it provides a highly accurate diagnostic benchmark to evaluate visual alert thresholds.

How to Use the Reaction Speed Checker

Follow these simple steps to measure your response times:

  1. Click or tap inside the large red "Waiting..." box to activate the test.
  2. Keep your focus on the box and wait patiently. The transition color delay is randomized between 1 and 5 seconds to prevent anticipation.
  3. As soon as the color shifts to bright green and displays "CLICK NOW!", click or tap the box immediately.
  4. Your reaction time will be displayed in milliseconds (ms).
  5. Click or tap again to restart and run multiple trials to find your average response profile.
  6. If you click too early while the screen is still red, the box will display a warning; click to reset and try again.

Key Features of the Reflex Benchmarker

  • Randomized Stimulus Intervals: Prevents rhythmic anticipation by varying the delay timing on each run.
  • Early Input Protection: Instantly detects and flags premature mouse clicks or screen taps.
  • Millisecond High-Precision Timers: Relies on the high-resolution browser performance clock API.
  • Local Personal Best: Caches your lowest reaction time to track your records over time.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is an average human reaction time?
The average human reaction time to visual stimuli is roughly 200 to 250 milliseconds. Well-trained esports athletes and individuals with high alertness often achieve scores below 180 ms.

Does my display hardware affect my test score?
Yes. Monitors with high input lag, slow panel response times, or lower refresh rates (like 60Hz) will add minor latency. Fast displays (like 144Hz+) and wired gaming mice yield better scores.

How does browser overhead impact my reaction metrics?
Browsers occasionally suffer from rendering drops. Keeping other browser tabs closed and closing heavy background applications ensures the most accurate, lag-free test.

Are sound-based reactions faster than visual reactions?
Yes. Human auditory reaction pathways are generally faster, averaging around 140 to 160 milliseconds, compared to the visual pathway which requires more complex retinal processing.
