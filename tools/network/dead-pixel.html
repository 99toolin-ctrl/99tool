What is the Infinite Dead Pixel Tester?

The Infinite Dead Pixel Tester is a professional, browser-based utility designed to analyze the health of digital screens. Modern monitors and smartphone displays consist of millions of independent red, green, and blue sub-pixels. This tool cycles through pure color backdrops, making it easy to identify defects in your screen's sub-pixel matrix.

In addition to standard color diagnostics, the tool includes a dedicated high-frequency stimulator. This feature flashes high-contrast color arrays rapidly to stimulate lazy sub-pixels and potentially restore uniform panel illumination without requiring physical pressure or software downloads.

How to Use the Dead Pixel Checker

Using the screen tester is simple and takes only a few minutes:

  1. Ensure your screen is clean and free of dust before starting.
  2. Click the "Start Full Screen Test" button to activate the overlay.
  3. Click or tap anywhere on the screen to cycle through the color presets.
  4. Examine every quadrant of the display closely under each solid color.
  5. Press the Escape key (ESC) or double-tap on mobile to close the full-screen view.
  6. If you spot a stuck pixel, click "Run Pixel Fixer" to start the rapid color-flashing loop.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between a dead and a stuck pixel?
A dead pixel is permanently dark (black) because its sub-pixels have lost power. A stuck pixel is stuck in the "on" state, showing as a permanent red, green, or blue dot.

How long should I run the Pixel Fixer?
We recommend running the flashing Pixel Fixer loop for 10 to 20 minutes. If the sub-pixel is merely stuck rather than physically broken, this stimulus may help restore normal function.

Is the flashing cycle safe for OLED displays?
Yes, the flashing cycles are safe. However, individuals with photosensitive epilepsy should avoid looking at the screen while the Pixel Fixer's rapid flashing is active.

Can this tool repair physical screen damage?
No, physical cracks, liquid leaks, or permanently dead pixels cannot be repaired by software, as they indicate physical circuit damage.
