What is Advanced Mouse Tester

The Advanced Mouse Tester is an interactive, browser-based hardware diagnostic tool designed to test your computer mouse buttons, scroll wheel, and optical sensor. By capturing and visualizing your hardware inputs, it allows you to identify issues like double-clicking switches, scroll-wheel jitter, or coordinate tracking drops without installing extra drivers.

This checker features a virtual 3D-styled mouse schematic that highlights buttons in real-time as physical clicks are registered. The interface keeps track of total clicks per key to help you audit switch consistency and identify mechanical failures.

How to Use Advanced Mouse Tester

  1. Hover to test tracking: Move your cursor inside the dark workspace block to track your X and Y coordinates. The coordinate indicators update dynamically to show sensor stability.
  2. Test left & right clicks: Click your left and right mouse buttons inside the workspace. The virtual mouse left/right buttons will light up in red, and the click counters will increment.
  3. Test middle scroll click: Click your scroll wheel button down to verify middle-click functionality. The middle wheel button on the schematic will respond.
  4. Test scroll wheel direction: Roll your scroll wheel up and down inside the workspace. The scroll-indicator box displays the scroll delta value, allowing you to test for scroll jumps or encoder failures.
  5. Inspect adjustment mode: Check the **DPR Adjusted** status card to verify if high-DPI coordinates are correctly rendered by your screen's device pixel ratio.

Key Features

  • Interactive Virtual Schematic: Visualizes left button, right button, and scroll wheel states instantly.
  • Scroll Wheel Delta Display: Measures the vertical displacement of scroll actions to detect loose encoders and scroll jumping.
  • Context Menu Override: Overrides standard browser right-click events inside the workspace, allowing you to test right-clicks without menus blocking your screen.
  • High-Precision Coordinates: Tracks coordinate changes across high-DPI displays with device pixel ratio scaling.

Common Use Cases

  • Gamers switch diagnostics: Verifying if mechanical gaming switches (like Omron, Cherry, or Kailh) suffer from double-click registration errors.
  • Scroll Wheel Encoder Cleaning: Testing if cleaning a dust-filled scroll wheel encoder has resolved scrolling glitches or reversed scrolls.
  • Office hardware verification: Checking newly purchased or storage-retrieved office mice before deploying them.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q: What causes double-clicking in mechanical mouse switches?
A: Double-clicking is usually caused by wear on the copper leaf spring inside the mechanical microswitch. Over time, metal fatigue or corrosion causes the contacts to bounce, sending two electrical signals for a single physical click.

Q: How do I identify scroll wheel encoder failure?
A: Roll your scroll wheel steadily in one direction (e.g., down). If the scroll delta display value jumps to positive numbers or fluctuates wildly, the internal encoder is failing to read the wheel's rotation steps.

Q: Does this tool support extra side buttons (like Forward and Back)?
A: This version is optimized for core mouse controls: Left, Right, Middle (Wheel click), and Scroll actions. Side buttons may trigger standard browser navigation rather than registering inside this sandbox.

Q: What does the "DPR Adjusted" value mean?
A: DPR stands for Device Pixel Ratio. High-resolution screens scale coordinate values to match CSS pixels. The tool confirms that these scaling factors are correctly applied to the coordinate outputs.

Privacy & Data Note

All button click events and cursor movements are handled locally in your browser's execution thread. Your hardware activity is never recorded, saved, or sent to external servers.
