What is the Infinite Body Fat Calculator?

The Infinite Body Fat Calculator is a client-side body composition utility designed to estimate body fat percentage using circumference measurements. Based on the widely recognized U.S. Navy Method, it uses height, neck, waist, and hip dimensions to calculate fat mass ratios without clinical scans.

This calculator processes all logarithmic formulas, circumference equations, and gender adjustments locally in your browser memory. Your measurements and calculated percentages are never uploaded, ensuring complete data security.

How to Use the Infinite Body Fat Calculator

  1. Select Gender: Choose Male or Female from the dropdown menu. Selecting Female displays the required Hips input box.
  2. Enter Height: Input your height in centimeters (cm).
  3. Measure & Enter Neck Circumference: Measure your neck just below the larynx and input the value.
  4. Measure & Enter Waist Circumference: Measure your waist horizontally at the navel level for men, or at the narrowest point for women.
  5. Measure & Enter Hip Circumference (Females Only): Measure the hips at the widest point and enter the value.
  6. Calculate Body Fat: Click the "Estimate Body Fat" button to run the formulas locally and view your results card.

Frequently Asked Questions

The formula uses logarithmic equations based on differences in circumferences:
For Men: 495 / (1.0324 - 0.19077 * log10(waist - neck) + 0.15456 * log10(height)) - 450
For Women: 495 / (1.29579 - 0.35004 * log10(waist + hip - neck) + 0.22100 * log10(height)) - 450

For most individuals, the U.S. Navy circumference method is accurate to within ±3% of clinical measurements like DXA scans or hydrostatic weighing, provided measurements are taken carefully.

Typical healthy ranges are 14% to 24% for men and 21% to 31% for women. Athletes often maintain lower ranges (6% to 13% for men and 14% to 20% for women).

Biological fat distribution patterns differ by gender. The formula accounts for these variations by using waist-to-neck measurements for men, and adding hip measurements for women.
